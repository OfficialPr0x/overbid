# 01 — Brand and voice

## Position
An ambitious, discreet marketplace and transaction experience for owners and acquisition buyers. The brand should feel like discovering a private archive of valuable business opportunities: modern wealth, deliberate access, excellent preparation. Trust comes from named accountable people, useful disclosures, clear roles, visible terms and demonstrable process.

## Visual vocabulary
- Rabbit: curiosity, watchfulness and a path into the unknown.
- Third eye: insight as metaphor for research and judgment.
- Broken triangle: threshold, direction and a distinctive frame.
- Keyhole: controlled information access, not blanket security promises.
- Black stone and fine orbital geometry: permanence and precision.
- Acid lime: contemporary founder culture and action.

The symbolism belongs in artwork and identity. Interface labels remain ordinary and understandable. No 'initiation', 'clearance level', 'secret returns', 'guaranteed protection', or unsupported 'verified buyer' badges.

## Personality
Confident, selective, culturally aware, quietly provocative. Use short declarative headlines and concrete supporting copy. Speak to owners as peers. Avoid crypto-bro jargon, excessive exclamation marks, guru claims, fake urgency, casino cues and contempt for inexperienced users. Authority without intimidation.

## Hierarchy
Headline may intrigue. Subcopy must say what the company does. Button must state the action. If someone cannot tell how to sell or browse within five seconds, the design fails.

## Naming discipline
Brand name OVERBID CAPITAL; body copy OVERBID. This does not establish a legal entity, investment-bank status, licensing or principal-acquisition capacity. Actual legal identity, role and fees require owner input. Distinguish advisory representation from marketplace listing and buying with company capital. Do not imply all three exist merely from the brand name.

## Approved linguistic patterns
'You built the empire. Own your next move.'
'Discretion outside. Clarity inside.'
'Follow the white rabbit.'
'Your business. Presented properly.'
'Find the business worth your next move.'

Do not turn every paragraph into mythology. Use mystery sparingly and answer real questions directly.
